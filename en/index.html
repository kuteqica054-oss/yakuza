  1. 01

    Families follow the kids

    When a child wants to play, the whole family comes. Parents shop, grab a coffee and stay longer — so the arena drives footfall for the whole floor, not just its own tickets.

  2. 02

    Birthdays and group bookings

    Birthday parties, school trips, sports clubs. Laser tag is a group activity, which means pre-booked, packageable revenue on your calendar.

  3. 03

    Built for repeat visits

    No two games end the same way. New missions, leaderboards and team rankings keep kids asking for one more round.

  4. 04

    Weatherproof, year-round

    Indoors and independent of season — as busy on a rainy November weekend as during the summer holidays.

  5. 05

    Strong use of floor space

    A maze layout lets many kids play at once on a compact footprint, with far more layout flexibility than large mechanical rides.

  6. 06

    Easy to run

    Games start from a single console; timing and scoring are automatic. Your host spends time with the kids, not the screen.

Safety

Kids first

Parents need peace of mind — and so do operators. Safety starts on line one of the design.

Eye-safe signal
Despite the name, our blasters use an infrared (IR) signal — the same kind of technology as a TV remote. Technical documentation on eye safety is handed over with the equipment.TODO: Verify against the actual equipment documentation and applicable standards.
Soft walls, rounded corners
Maze walls use impact-absorbing materials and rounded edges, so there are no sharp corners for running kids.TODO: Add material details.
Exits always visible
Whatever the game lighting, emergency exit and guidance lights stay visible. House lights come on with one switch.
Age-based game modes
Calmer, points-based games for younger kids; strategy missions for older ones.
Host-supervised play
Every game starts with a short briefing. The host watches the game, reminds kids of the rules and can stop play with one tap.
